A cyclone makes landfall on the Odisha coast while freight moves from Kalinganagar to Paradip, Dhamra and Gopalpur. The same storm and the same trucks run twice: on the left, dispatch with no map intelligence; on the right, dispatch guided by a geospatial forecast of rain, flooded road segments and port queues.

Illustrative model. District boundaries are real (2011 census districts). The storm, road alignments, flood-proneness, port capacities and traffic are assumed, and road lines are schematic, not survey-grade. Do not use any number here outside this page without real data.

How the model works
  • Storm: the centre moves from the Bay of Bengal to landfall near Paradip and Dhamra at the hour you choose, then weakens inland. Rain peaks in a ring about 50 km from the centre, with a broad lighter band around it. Severity scales the rain.
  • Roads: each segment slows with rain at that location. If the last 8 hours of rain, scaled by that segment’s flood-proneness, pass a threshold, the segment is waterlogged and trucks crawl at about 12% of normal speed until the water clears. Coastal segments near the ports are the most flood-prone.
  • Ports: gate capacity is 40 trucks an hour at Paradip, 28 at Dhamra and 16 at Gopalpur, reduced by up to 75% in heavy rain at the port. Cargo is split 55%, 30% and 15% between them.
  • Without GeoAI: each truck leaves when ready and takes its usual road to its usual port, meeting whatever the storm has done by then.
  • With GeoAI: tracked trucks are checked every 15 minutes. For each road and port option the model predicts the drive time, using the forecast rain on every segment, plus the predicted wait at the gate. A truck is held at the plant yard (engine off) if the best option is still more than an hour slower than free flow, for up to 8 hours. Cargo that can switch may go to another port, and Paradip trucks may use the Cuttack road when the direct road is predicted to flood. Loads bound for Gopalpur stay there.
  • Forecast accuracy: the forecast knows the real storm but places its track with an error that grows with lead time. At 100% accuracy the error is zero; at 0% it is about 300 km a day, so the model barely helps beyond what the queues already show.
  • Fuel: 0.58 litres of diesel per route kilometre for the round trip plus 2.5 litres an hour idling at a gate.

Both runs use the same trucks, so any difference comes from the dispatch decisions alone. A new random run draws new trucks and a new forecast error direction. Trucks are not rerouted once they are on the road, which a real system would also do.
